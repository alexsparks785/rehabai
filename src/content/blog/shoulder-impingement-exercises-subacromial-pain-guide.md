# Shoulder Impingement Exercises: Complete Subacromial Pain Relief Guide

Shoulder impingement—also called subacromial pain syndrome—is the most common cause of shoulder pain, affecting up to 44% of all shoulder complaints. That pinching pain when you raise your arm overhead can make daily activities miserable and sideline athletes from their sports.

The good news: research strongly supports exercise as first-line treatment, with outcomes equal to or better than surgery for most cases.

## Understanding Shoulder Impingement

### What's Happening

The subacromial space is the area between your rotator cuff tendons and the bony arch above them (acromion). Impingement occurs when structures in this space get compressed or irritated during arm movement.

**Tissues involved:**
- Rotator cuff tendons (especially supraspinatus)
- Subacromial bursa
- Long head of biceps tendon

### Why It Happens

**Structural factors:**
- Hooked or curved acromion shape
- Bone spurs
- Thickened ligaments

**Functional factors (more important):**
- Rotator cuff weakness
- Poor scapular control
- Tight posterior shoulder
- Poor posture (rounded shoulders)
- Overuse or training errors

### Symptoms

- Pain with overhead reaching
- Pain lying on affected side
- Painful arc (pain mid-range, 60-120°)
- Weakness with arm elevation
- Night pain
- Difficulty reaching behind back
- Gradual onset (usually)

## Why Exercise Works

Research shows exercise produces outcomes equal to surgery for most shoulder impingement, with lower risk and cost.

**Exercise addresses:**
1. Rotator cuff strength and function
2. Scapular muscle balance
3. Posterior shoulder tightness
4. Movement patterns and posture

**What exercise can't change:**
- Bone shape (but matters less than function)
- Significant structural damage

## Phase 1: Pain Management (Weeks 1-2)

### Activity Modification

**Avoid or modify:**
- Overhead reaching
- Sleeping on affected side
- Pressing movements
- Repetitive shoulder use

**Continue:**
- Lower body exercise
- Pain-free movements
- Gentle range of motion

### Pendulum Exercises

Classic first-line exercise—gentle motion without muscle activation.

**Pendulum**
1. Lean over, support with good arm on table
2. Let affected arm hang
3. Gently swing in circles
4. Let momentum do the work
5. 1-2 minutes, multiple times daily

### Gentle Range of Motion

**Supine Flexion**
1. Lie on back
2. Use good arm to lift affected arm overhead
3. Go only to comfortable range
4. 10-15 repetitions

**Table Slides**
1. Sit at table, forearm on towel
2. Slide arm forward, letting body lean
3. Gentle stretch at end range
4. 10-15 repetitions

### Posterior Shoulder Stretching

Tightness here contributes to impingement.

**Cross-Body Stretch**
1. Bring affected arm across body
2. Use other hand to pull elbow toward opposite shoulder
3. Keep shoulder down (don't shrug)
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. 3-5 repetitions

**Sleeper Stretch**
1. Lie on affected side
2. Shoulder and elbow at 90°
3. Use other hand to push forearm toward floor
4. Gentle stretch in back of shoulder
5. Hold 30 seconds
6. Controversial—skip if painful

## Phase 2: Rotator Cuff Strengthening (Weeks 2-8)

### External Rotation (Priority Exercise)

**Side-Lying External Rotation**
1. Lie on unaffected side
2. Affected elbow at side, bent 90°
3. Rotate forearm toward ceiling
4. Lower slowly
5. 3 sets of 15
6. Add light weight as tolerated

**Standing External Rotation with Band**
1. Band anchored at waist height
2. Elbow at side, bent 90°
3. Rotate forearm outward
4. Keep elbow pinned to side
5. 3 sets of 15

**90/90 External Rotation**
1. Arm out to side at 90°, elbow bent 90°
2. Rotate forearm from pointing down to pointing up
3. Use light band or weight
4. 3 sets of 15
5. Progress to this position gradually

### Internal Rotation

**Standing Internal Rotation with Band**
1. Band anchored at waist height
2. Elbow at side, bent 90°
3. Rotate forearm across body
4. Control return
5. 3 sets of 15

### Supraspinatus (Careful Progression)

**Full Can (Preferred Over Empty Can)**
1. Stand with arm at side, thumb pointing up
2. Raise arm forward and slightly out (about 30° from midline)
3. Stop at shoulder height or below pain
4. Lower slowly
5. 3 sets of 15
6. Light weight only

### Scaption

**Scaption Raises**
1. Arms at sides, thumbs up
2. Raise arms in plane of scapula (30-45° forward from side)
3. Stop at or below shoulder height
4. 3 sets of 15
5. Often better tolerated than frontal or lateral raises

## Phase 3: Scapular Strengthening (Weeks 2-8)

Poor scapular control is a major contributor to impingement.

### Lower Trapezius (Often Weak)

**Prone Y Raise**
1. Lie face down, arms hanging off table/bench
2. Raise arms in Y position (45° from head)
3. Thumbs pointing up
4. Squeeze shoulder blades
5. 3 sets of 15

**Prone T Raise**
1. Same position
2. Raise arms straight out to sides
3. Thumbs up
4. 3 sets of 15

**Wall Slide**
1. Back against wall, arms in "goalpost" position
2. Slide arms up wall
3. Keep back flat against wall
4. 3 sets of 10

### Serratus Anterior

**Wall Push-Up Plus**
1. Hands on wall, push-up position
2. Do push-up, then push further (protract scapulae)
3. "Plus" is the extra push at end
4. 3 sets of 15

**Serratus Punches**
1. Lie on back, arm toward ceiling with light weight
2. Punch toward ceiling (shoulder blade comes off floor)
3. Lower shoulder blade
4. 3 sets of 15

**Bear Position Protraction**
1. On hands and knees
2. Push floor away, rounding upper back
3. Let shoulder blades separate
4. Hold 5 seconds
5. 10 repetitions

### Middle Trapezius/Rhomboids

**Prone W Raise**
1. Lie face down
2. Bend elbows, squeeze shoulder blades together
3. Form "W" shape with arms
4. Hold 3 seconds
5. 3 sets of 15

**Band Pull-Apart**
1. Hold band in front at shoulder height
2. Pull band apart by squeezing shoulder blades
3. Keep arms straight
4. 3 sets of 15-20

**Rows (Any Variation)**
1. Cable, band, or dumbbell
2. Pull to waist, squeeze shoulder blades
3. 3 sets of 12-15

## Phase 4: Integration and Function (Weeks 6-12)

### Push-Up Progression

**Wall Push-Up Plus**
1. Progress from wall to incline to floor
2. Always include "plus" at top
3. Full scapular movement

**Push-Up with Scapular Control**
1. Standard push-up
2. Let shoulder blades come together at bottom
3. Push apart at top
4. 3 sets of 10-15

### Overhead Progression

**Supine Overhead Press**
1. Lie on back, press weights straight up
2. Start position: often better tolerated
3. 3 sets of 12

**Landmine Press**
1. Barbell in corner or landmine attachment
2. Press at angle (not fully overhead)
3. 3 sets of 10-12 each arm
4. Transitional exercise to overhead

**Overhead Press (When Ready)**
1. Dumbbells often better than barbell
2. Slight forward angle may help
3. Start light, progress slowly
4. 3 sets of 10-12

### Pulling Movements

**Face Pull**
1. Cable or band at face height
2. Pull toward face, elbows high
3. External rotate at end
4. 3 sets of 15-20

**High Row**
1. Pull toward upper chest
2. Elbows out to sides
3. 3 sets of 12

## Sample Weekly Programs

### Weeks 1-2: Pain Management
**Daily:**
- Pendulum exercises: 2-3 min
- Gentle ROM: 10-15 reps
- Cross-body stretch: 3 × 30 sec
- Ice as needed

### Weeks 2-6: Rotator Cuff Focus
**Daily:**
- Stretching routine

**4× weekly:**
- External rotation: 3 × 15
- Internal rotation: 3 × 15
- Scaption: 3 × 15
- Y-T-W (prone): 3 × 15 each
- Serratus work: 3 × 15

### Weeks 6-12: Integration
**4× weekly:**
- Rotator cuff maintenance: 2 sets each
- Scapular strengthening: 2-3 exercises
- Push-up progression: 3 × 10-15
- Pulling movements: 3 × 12-15
- Overhead progression (when ready)

### Maintenance
**3× weekly:**
- Rotator cuff exercises
- Scapular strengthening
- Balanced push/pull training

## Posture Considerations

### Desk Posture

**Address:**
- Forward head position
- Rounded shoulders
- Thoracic kyphosis

**Strategies:**
- Monitor at eye level
- Keyboard at elbow height
- Regular breaks
- Chin tucks throughout day

### Thoracic Extension

**Foam Roller Extension**
1. Roller perpendicular to spine (upper back)
2. Support head with hands
3. Extend over roller
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. Move roller to different levels

**Cat-Cow**
1. On hands and knees
2. Arch and round spine
3. Focus on thoracic movement
4. 10 repetitions

**Thread the Needle**
1. On hands and knees
2. Reach one arm under body, rotating
3. Follow with eyes
4. 10 each side

## Common Mistakes

### Exercise Errors

**Working through sharp pain:**
- Muscle fatigue okay, sharp pain not okay
- Modify or skip painful exercises

**Going too heavy too fast:**
- Rotator cuff is small muscles
- Light weights, high reps initially

**Neglecting scapular muscles:**
- Often forgotten
- Critical for long-term success

**Stretching into impingement:**
- Some stretches worsen symptoms
- Modify or eliminate if painful

### Training Errors

**Too much overhead work:**
- Balance push/pull
- Prioritize horizontal pressing initially

**Ignoring posture:**
- Exercises alone may not be enough
- Daily postural awareness

## When to Seek Help

**See a healthcare provider if:**
- Severe pain limiting daily activities
- Weakness that doesn't improve
- Night pain preventing sleep
- Trauma or sudden onset
- No improvement after 6-8 weeks

**Consider imaging if:**
- Suspected full-thickness tear
- Symptoms not responding to exercise
- Significant weakness
- History of dislocation

## Key Takeaways

1. **Exercise equals or beats surgery** for most shoulder impingement
2. **External rotation is priority** — strengthens the rotator cuff
3. **Scapular muscles matter** — especially lower trap and serratus
4. **Posterior shoulder stretching** — addresses tightness contributing to impingement
5. **Progress overhead work gradually** — earn the right to press overhead
6. **Address posture** — rounded shoulders perpetuate the problem
7. **Be patient** — 8-12 weeks for significant improvement
8. **Maintenance is key** — prevent recurrence with continued exercise

Shoulder impingement responds very well to proper exercise. Focus on rotator cuff and scapular strengthening, address mobility restrictions, and progress gradually. Most people see significant improvement within 8-12 weeks of consistent work.
