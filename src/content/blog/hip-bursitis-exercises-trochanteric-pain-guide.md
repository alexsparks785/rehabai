# Hip Bursitis Exercises: Complete Trochanteric Pain Relief Guide

Hip bursitis—now more accurately called Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (GTPS)—causes pain on the outside of the hip that can make sleeping, walking, and climbing stairs miserable. This common condition affects up to 25% of women and 8% of men at some point in their lives.

The good news: research shows that targeted exercises, particularly hip strengthening, are more effective than steroid injections for long-term relief.

## Understanding Hip Bursitis / GTPS

### What's Actually Happening

The traditional view blamed inflammation of the bursa (fluid-filled sac) over the greater trochanter (bony bump on outer hip). We now understand it's usually more complex:

**Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome involves:**
- Gluteal tendinopathy (tendon degeneration)
- Bursal irritation
- IT band tightness/friction
- Muscle imbalances

This is why the condition is now called GTPS rather than just "bursitis."

### Common Causes

**Biomechanical:**
- Weak hip abductors (gluteus medius)
- Tight IT band
- Poor pelvic control
- Leg length discrepancy
- Gait abnormalities

**Overuse:**
- Sudden increase in walking/running
- Repetitive hip flexion
- Climbing stairs or hills
- Standing on one leg repeatedly

**Other factors:**
- Female sex (wider pelvis, different hip angles)
- Age (40-60 peak incidence)
- Obesity
- Previous hip surgery
- Low back problems

### Symptoms

- Pain on outer hip (over the "hip bone")
- Pain lying on affected side
- Pain climbing stairs
- Pain walking, especially longer distances
- Tenderness to pressure over greater trochanter
- Pain crossing legs
- Pain getting up from sitting
- Sometimes radiates down outer thigh

### GTPS vs. Hip Arthritis

| Feature | GTPS | Hip Arthritis |
|---------|------|---------------|
| Pain location | Outer hip | Groin, front of hip |
| Lying on side | Very painful | Usually okay |
| Pressing on bone | Tender | Not tender |
| Hip rotation | Usually fine | Limited, painful |

## Why Strengthening Works Better Than Injections

Research (including the LEAP trial) showed that:
- Education + exercise produced better 1-year outcomes than corticosteroid injection
- Injections help short-term but effects wear off
- Strengthening addresses the underlying cause (weak glutes)
- Exercise improvements are more durable

## Phase 1: Pain Management (Weeks 1-2)

### Activity Modification

**Avoid or modify:**
- Lying on affected side
- Crossing legs
- Standing with weight on affected leg
- Excessive stair climbing
- Prolonged standing

**Sleeping positions:**
- Lie on unaffected side with pillow between knees
- Or lie on back with pillow under knees
- If must sleep on affected side, use thick mattress topper

### Ice and Heat

**Ice:**
- 15-20 minutes over painful area
- After aggravating activities
- Can help with acute flares

**Heat:**
- Before exercises
- For muscle relaxation
- Some prefer heat over ice

### Gentle Stretching (Cautious)

**Important:** Aggressive IT band stretching can worsen GTPS by compressing the bursa/tendons. Be gentle.

**Modified Piriformis Stretch**
1. Lie on back, affected ankle over opposite knee
2. Gently pull bottom knee toward chest
3. Only go to mild stretch, not pain
4. Hold 30 seconds

**Hip Flexor Stretch**
1. Half-kneeling, affected knee down
2. Tuck pelvis under
3. Gentle shift forward
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. Don't overstretch

**Avoid:** Aggressive cross-body stretches, deep IT band stretches that compress the hip.

### Isometric Hip Abduction

Pain-relieving exercise that doesn't compress the bursa.

**Wall Isometric**
1. Stand sideways to wall, affected hip toward wall
2. Push outer hip/thigh into wall
3. Hold 30-45 seconds
4. 5 repetitions
5. 2-3 times daily

## Phase 2: Hip Strengthening (Weeks 2-8)

This is the most critical phase. Weak gluteus medius is the primary culprit.

### Gluteus Medius Strengthening (Priority)

**Side-Lying Hip Abduction**
1. Lie on unaffected side
2. Keep affected leg straight, toes pointing forward (not up)
3. Raise leg toward ceiling (not forward)
4. Lower slowly
5. 3 sets of 15-20
6. Avoid letting leg drift forward

**Clamshell**
1. Lie on unaffected side, knees bent 45°
2. Keep feet together
3. Raise top knee, don't let pelvis roll back
4. 3 sets of 20
5. Add resistance band to progress

**Standing Hip Abduction**
1. Stand holding wall, affected leg unsupported
2. Raise leg out to side (not forward or back)
3. Keep pelvis level
4. 3 sets of 15
5. Add ankle weight to progress

**Hip Hike (Pelvic Drop)**
1. Stand on step, affected leg on step
2. Let other hip drop down
3. Use stance-side glute to hike hip back up
4. 3 sets of 15
5. Excellent for gluteus medius

### Gluteus Maximus Strengthening

**Glute Bridge**
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat
2. Squeeze glutes, lift hips
3. Don't hyperextend back
4. Hold 3 seconds, lower slowly
5. 3 sets of 15-20

**Single-Leg Bridge (Progress To)**
1. Same position, one leg extended
2. Bridge on single leg
3. Keep pelvis level
4. 3 sets of 10-12 each side

**Hip Thrust**
1. Upper back on bench
2. Drive hips up by squeezing glutes
3. 3 sets of 12-15
4. Add weight to progress

### Core Strengthening

**Dead Bug**
1. Lie on back, arms up, knees at 90°
2. Lower opposite arm and leg
3. Keep low back pressed to floor
4. 3 sets of 10 each side

**Side Plank (Modified)**
1. On elbow and knees
2. Keep body in line
3. Hold 20-30 seconds
4. 3 each side
5. Progress to full side plank

**Bird Dog**
1. On hands and knees
2. Extend opposite arm and leg
3. Hold 3-5 seconds
4. 3 sets of 10 each side

### Hip External Rotation

**Seated External Rotation**
1. Sit with band around knees
2. Press knees apart
3. Hold 5 seconds
4. 3 sets of 15

**Side-Lying External Rotation**
1. Lie on unaffected side
2. Hip and knee bent 90°
3. Keep knee in place, rotate foot toward ceiling
4. 3 sets of 15

## Phase 3: Functional Strengthening (Weeks 6-12)

### Single-Leg Exercises

**Single-Leg Balance**
1. Stand on affected leg
2. Hold 30-60 seconds
3. Progress: eyes closed, unstable surface

**Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift**
1. Stand on affected leg
2. Hinge forward, other leg extends back
3. Keep pelvis level
4. 3 sets of 10 each side

**Step-Ups**
1. Step onto box with affected leg
2. Drive through that leg
3. Lower with control
4. 3 sets of 12

**Lateral Step-Down**
1. Stand on step, affected leg on step
2. Slowly lower other foot toward ground
3. Don't let hip drop—control with glute
4. 3 sets of 10 each side
5. Excellent functional exercise

### Walking/Running Progression

**Return to walking:**
- Start with shorter distances
- Flat terrain first
- Progress gradually (10% per week)
- Stop if pain increases during or after

**Return to running (if applicable):**
- Walking program first
- Walk-run intervals
- Flat terrain initially
- Address any gait issues

## Sample Programs

### Weeks 1-2: Pain Management
**Daily:**
- Activity modification
- Sleep positioning
- Isometric hip abduction: 5 × 45 sec
- Gentle stretching: 30 sec each (not aggressive)
- Ice as needed

### Weeks 2-6: Strengthening Focus
**4× weekly:**
- Side-lying hip abduction: 3 × 15
- Clamshell: 3 × 20
- Hip hike: 3 × 15
- Glute bridge: 3 × 15
- Core exercises: 2-3 exercises

**Daily:**
- Isometric maintenance
- Gentle stretching

### Weeks 6-12: Functional Progression
**4× weekly:**
- Single-leg balance: 3 × 30 sec
- Single-leg RDL: 3 × 10
- Step-ups: 3 × 12
- Lateral step-downs: 3 × 10
- Continue glute strengthening

### Maintenance
**3× weekly:**
- Hip strengthening circuit
- Core exercises

**Daily:**
- Awareness of positions/activities

## What NOT to Do

### Avoid These Stretches (Can Worsen GTPS)

**Aggressive IT band stretching:**
- Cross-body stretches that compress outer hip
- Standing IT band stretch (crossing legs)
- Foam rolling directly on the painful area

**Why:** These positions compress the bursa and tendons against the greater trochanter.

### Avoid These Positions

- Lying on affected side (or use padding)
- Crossing legs when sitting
- Standing with weight on affected leg
- Hip adduction (leg crossing midline)

### Avoid Overtraining

- Don't push through significant pain
- Progress gradually
- Allow recovery between sessions

## Foam Rolling Guidelines

**Yes (helpful):**
- Gluteus maximus (buttock)
- Quadriceps
- Hip flexors
- IT band above and below the painful area

**No (can aggravate):**
- Directly over the greater trochanter (painful spot)
- Prolonged rolling on outer hip

## When Exercises Aren't Enough

### Corticosteroid Injection

**May help if:**
- Severe pain limiting function
- Need short-term relief to participate in exercise
- Exercise alone not improving symptoms

**Limitations:**
- Effects often temporary (weeks to months)
- May need repeat injections
- Long-term: exercise produces better outcomes

### Shockwave Therapy

**Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT):**
- Emerging evidence for GTPS
- Typically 3-5 sessions
- May help with tendinopathy component

### Surgery

**Rarely needed:**
- Consider only after extensive conservative treatment fails
- Options: bursectomy, IT band release, gluteal tendon repair
- Recovery can be prolonged

## Prevention

### Maintain Hip Strength

- Continue exercises even when pain-free
- Especially gluteus medius
- 2-3× weekly maintenance

### Activity Management

- Progress walking/running gradually
- Vary activities
- Avoid sudden increases in load

### Ergonomics

- Avoid prolonged standing on one leg
- Sit with legs uncrossed
- Use proper sleeping position

### Body Weight

- Maintaining healthy weight reduces hip load
- Gradual weight loss if overweight

## When to See a Doctor

**Soon:**
- Pain not improving after 6-8 weeks
- Significant night pain
- Pain at rest (not just with activity)
- Difficulty walking

**Rule out other conditions:**
- Hip arthritis (groin pain, limited rotation)
- Lumbar radiculopathy (back-related leg pain)
- Stress fracture (if runner with worsening pain)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Strengthening beats injections** long-term (research-proven)
2. **Gluteus medius is the key** — focus on hip abduction exercises
3. **Avoid compressive positions** — lying on affected side, crossing legs
4. **Don't over-stretch the IT band** — can make it worse
5. **Sleep modifications matter** — pillow between knees
6. **Progress single-leg work** — functional exercises are important
7. **8-12 weeks for improvement** — be patient
8. **Maintenance is essential** — prevent recurrence with continued exercise

Hip bursitis/GTPS can be stubborn, but the evidence strongly supports exercise as the best treatment. Focus on hip strengthening (especially gluteus medius), avoid compressive positions, and be patient. Most people see significant improvement within 2-3 months of consistent work.
