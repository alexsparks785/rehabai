# Achilles Tendinitis Exercises: Complete Recovery and Prevention Guide

Achilles tendinitis is one of the most common overuse injuries, affecting runners, recreational athletes, and even sedentary individuals. The Achilles tendon—the largest and strongest tendon in the body—connects your calf muscles to your heel, and when it becomes painful and irritated, every step can become a challenge.

The good news: research strongly supports specific exercises, particularly eccentric loading, as the most effective treatment. This guide covers the evidence-based approach to Achilles tendon recovery.

## Understanding Achilles Tendinopathy

### Tendinitis vs. Tendinopathy

While commonly called "tendinitis" (implying inflammation), most Achilles tendon pain is actually "tendinopathy"—a degenerative condition involving:
- Failed healing response
- Disorganized collagen fibers
- Minimal inflammation in chronic cases

This distinction matters because it explains why anti-inflammatory treatments provide only temporary relief, while loading exercises actually heal the tendon.

### Types of Achilles Tendinopathy

**Midportion Tendinopathy (Most Common)**
- Pain 2-6 cm above heel bone
- Often includes visible thickening
- Responds well to eccentric exercises

**Insertional Tendinopathy**
- Pain where tendon attaches to heel bone
- Often involves bone spur (Haglund's deformity)
- May require modified exercise approach
- Can be aggravated by heel drop exercises

### Common Causes
- Rapid increase in activity
- Training errors (too much, too soon)
- Inadequate recovery
- Tight calf muscles
- Weak calf muscles
- Poor footwear
- Running on hills or uneven surfaces
- Age-related tendon changes

### Symptoms
- Pain and stiffness in morning
- Pain that warms up with activity initially
- Pain after prolonged activity
- Tenderness to touch
- Swelling or thickening
- Decreased ankle flexibility
- Weakness with pushing off

## Why Eccentric Loading Works

The Alfredson Protocol—eccentric heel drops—revolutionized Achilles tendinopathy treatment. Research shows eccentric exercises:

1. **Stimulate tendon remodeling** — Creates healthier collagen organization
2. **Increase tendon strength** — Builds capacity to handle load
3. **Reduce pain** — Through neurological and structural changes
4. **Promote healing** — Better blood flow and cellular activity

Studies show 60-90% of patients improve significantly with eccentric exercise programs.

## Phase 1: Pain Management (Week 1-2)

### Relative Rest (Not Complete Rest)

Complete rest leads to tendon weakening. Instead:
- Reduce aggravating activities
- Avoid painful movements
- Stay active with non-painful alternatives (swimming, cycling)
- Walk as tolerated

### Isometric Loading

Isometrics reduce pain and maintain tendon capacity.

**Seated Calf Raise Isometric**
1. Sit with feet flat on floor
2. Raise heels off ground
3. Hold 30-45 seconds
4. Pain should be ≤3/10
5. 5 holds, 3 times daily

**Standing Calf Raise Isometric**
1. Stand on both feet
2. Rise onto toes
3. Hold 30-45 seconds
4. Use wall or chair for balance
5. 5 holds, 3 times daily

### Gentle Stretching

**Standing Calf Stretch (Gastrocnemius)**
1. Stand facing wall, hands on wall
2. Step affected leg back, keep heel down
3. Keep back knee straight
4. Lean forward until stretch felt in upper calf
5. Hold 30 seconds
6. 3-5 repetitions, multiple times daily

**Bent-Knee Calf Stretch (Soleus)**
1. Same position as above
2. Bend back knee while keeping heel down
3. Feel stretch lower in calf, near Achilles
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. 3-5 repetitions

### Avoid

- High-impact activities
- Running or jumping
- Hill walking
- Excessive stretching into pain
- Completely flat shoes (some heel helps reduce strain)

## Phase 2: Eccentric Loading Protocol (Weeks 2-12)

The Alfredson Protocol is the gold standard for Achilles tendinopathy.

### The Alfredson Protocol

**Equipment needed:** Step, stair, or raised surface

**Straight-Knee Eccentric Heel Drop**
1. Stand on edge of step with balls of feet, heels hanging off
2. Rise up onto toes using both feet
3. Lift good foot off step
4. Slowly lower affected heel below step level over 3-5 seconds
5. Put good foot back down and rise up using both feet
6. Repeat

**Key points:**
- Use both legs to go UP (concentric)
- Use only affected leg to go DOWN (eccentric)
- Lower slowly and controlled
- Go below the level of the step

**Bent-Knee Eccentric Heel Drop**
1. Same setup
2. Keep knee slightly bent throughout
3. Targets soleus muscle specifically
4. Same technique: up with both, down with one

### Original Alfredson Protocol

**Volume:**
- 3 sets of 15 repetitions (straight knee)
- 3 sets of 15 repetitions (bent knee)
- Twice daily
- Total: 180 heel drops per day

**Duration:**
- 12 weeks minimum
- Continue even if pain-free
- Don't stop early despite improvement

**Progression:**
- Start with bodyweight
- Add weight (backpack, weighted vest) when bodyweight becomes easy
- Increase load in 5 lb increments
- Pain during exercise: 3-5/10 is acceptable

### Modified Protocol (If Standard Is Too Aggressive)

**Reduced Volume:**
- 3 sets of 10 repetitions
- Once daily initially
- Progress to full protocol

**Bilateral Start:**
- Lower with both feet initially
- Progress to single-leg as tolerated

**Floor-Based:**
- Heel raises on flat ground
- Progress to step when ready

### For Insertional Tendinopathy

The hanging heel position can irritate insertional issues. Modify by:

**No Overhang Protocol:**
1. Perform on flat ground only
2. Rise onto toes, lower to floor (not below)
3. Same sets/reps as standard protocol
4. Progress to small overhang as tolerated

**Seated Heel Raises:**
1. Sit with weights on thighs
2. Rise onto toes, lower slowly
3. Alternative when standing is too painful

## Phase 3: Strengthening and Load Progression (Weeks 8-16)

As pain decreases, add concentric work and progress loading.

### Calf Strengthening

**Full Range Calf Raises**
1. Stand on step, both feet
2. Rise up AND lower down with both feet
3. Full range: above step, below step
4. 3 sets of 15-20
5. Progress to single-leg

**Single-Leg Calf Raises (Floor)**
1. Stand on affected leg
2. Rise onto toes
3. Lower with control
4. 3 sets of 15
5. Use wall for balance
6. Progress to step

**Seated Calf Raises**
1. Sit with dumbbells or barbell on thighs
2. Rise onto toes
3. Lower slowly
4. 3 sets of 15-20
5. Targets soleus specifically

### Ankle Strengthening

**Resistance Band Dorsiflexion**
1. Sit with leg extended, band around foot
2. Pull toes toward shin against resistance
3. 3 sets of 15

**Resistance Band Inversion/Eversion**
1. Band around foot
2. Turn foot in (inversion) and out (eversion)
3. 3 sets of 15 each direction

### Progressive Loading

**Principle:** Gradually increase what the tendon can handle

**Progression variables:**
- Add weight to heel drops
- Progress to single-leg work
- Increase speed (with control)
- Add plyometrics (Phase 4)

## Phase 4: Return to Activity (Weeks 12+)

### Plyometric Progression

Progress slowly—each phase for 1-2 weeks minimum.

**Level 1: Jump Rope (Double Leg)**
1. Light bouncing on both feet
2. 2-3 minutes
3. Pain should stay ≤2/10 during and after

**Level 2: Double-Leg Hops**
1. Small vertical hops in place
2. Soft landing
3. 3 sets of 10

**Level 3: Single-Leg Hops**
1. Small hops on affected leg
2. Focus on controlled landing
3. 3 sets of 8-10

**Level 4: Depth Drops**
1. Step off low box
2. Land softly on both feet
3. Progress height gradually

### Running Progression

**Criteria to start running:**
- Pain ≤2/10 with daily activities
- Single-leg heel raise: 25+ reps without pain
- Single-leg hop: 10+ reps without pain
- Walking 30+ minutes without symptoms

**Return to run protocol:**

*Week 1:*
- Walk 4 min, jog 1 min
- Repeat 4 times
- Every other day

*Week 2:*
- Walk 3 min, jog 2 min
- Repeat 4 times

*Week 3:*
- Walk 2 min, jog 3 min
- Repeat 4 times

*Week 4:*
- Walk 1 min, jog 4 min
- Repeat 4 times

Progress based on symptoms. Any increase in pain → back off one week.

## Sample Weekly Programs

### Weeks 1-2: Pain Management
**Daily:**
- Isometric calf holds: 5 × 45 sec
- Gentle stretching: 3 × 30 sec each
- Ice after activity if inflamed
- Relative rest from aggravating activities

### Weeks 2-12: Eccentric Loading
**Twice daily:**
- Alfredson Protocol: 3 × 15 straight knee + 3 × 15 bent knee

**Daily:**
- Stretching: 3 × 30 sec each
- Continue non-painful activities

### Weeks 8-16: Strengthening
**Daily:**
- Eccentric protocol (can reduce to once daily)
- Calf raises: 3 × 15-20

**3× weekly:**
- Full calf strengthening routine
- Ankle strengthening
- Begin plyometric progression (when ready)

### Weeks 12+: Return to Activity
**Ongoing:**
- Maintenance eccentric exercises (3×/week minimum)
- Sport-specific progression
- Running protocol if applicable

## Footwear and Orthotics

### Shoe Considerations
- Adequate heel drop (8-12mm) can reduce Achilles strain
- Firm heel counter for stability
- Replace worn shoes regularly
- Avoid completely flat shoes during recovery

### Heel Lifts
- Temporary heel lift (5-10mm) can reduce strain
- Use in both shoes to prevent imbalance
- Gradually wean off as symptoms improve

### Custom Orthotics
- May help if biomechanical issues contribute
- Not necessary for everyone
- Consider if flat feet, overpronation, or other factors

## Common Mistakes

### Training Errors
- **Stopping too early:** Protocol requires 12 weeks
- **Avoiding all pain:** Mild discomfort (3-5/10) during exercise is acceptable
- **Too much too soon:** Gradual progression is essential
- **Inconsistency:** Daily exercises are crucial

### Exercise Errors
- **Going too fast:** Slow, controlled lowering is key
- **Not going below step level:** Full stretch is needed
- **Using affected leg to rise:** Both legs up, one leg down
- **Skipping bent-knee version:** Targets different muscle

### Recovery Errors
- **Complete rest:** Weakens tendon further
- **Returning too quickly:** Risk of re-injury
- **Ignoring morning stiffness:** Important progress indicator
- **Not addressing cause:** Training errors, footwear, etc.

## When to Seek Help

**See a healthcare provider if:**
- Severe pain or sudden onset
- Swelling that doesn't resolve
- No improvement after 6-8 weeks of exercise
- Significant weakness
- Pain that worsens with protocol
- Any popping sensation followed by pain (possible rupture)

**Consider physical therapy for:**
- Supervised progression
- Manual therapy
- Gait analysis
- Running form assessment

## Prevention Strategies

### Training Principles
- Increase mileage/intensity by max 10% per week
- Include rest days
- Vary training surfaces
- Don't ignore early warning signs

### Calf Maintenance
- Regular calf strengthening (even when healthy)
- Eccentric exercises 2-3× weekly
- Stretching after activity
- Self-massage or foam rolling

### Footwear
- Replace shoes every 300-500 miles
- Appropriate shoe for your foot type
- Gradual transition to new shoes

## Key Takeaways

1. **Achilles tendinopathy is tendinosis** — needs loading, not just rest
2. **Eccentric heel drops are the gold standard** — Alfredson Protocol
3. **12 weeks minimum** — don't stop early even if improved
4. **Pain of 3-5/10 during exercise is acceptable** — higher means modify
5. **Both straight and bent knee exercises** — target different muscles
6. **Progression is gradual** — bodyweight → weighted → plyometrics → running
7. **Address the cause** — training errors, footwear, biomechanics
8. **Maintenance matters** — continue exercises to prevent recurrence

Achilles tendinopathy can be stubborn, but the eccentric loading protocol has excellent research support. Commit to the full 12-week program, be consistent, and most people see significant improvement. Your tendon can heal—it just needs the right stimulus.
