# Hip Impingement Exercises: Complete FAI Recovery Guide

Hip impingement—medically called femoroacetabular impingement (FAI)—occurs when the ball and socket of your hip joint don't fit together perfectly, causing abnormal contact during movement. This common condition affects up to 15% of the population and is a frequent cause of hip and groin pain in active adults.

The good news: research shows that most people with hip impingement can significantly improve with targeted exercises and movement modifications, often avoiding surgery entirely.

## Understanding Hip Impingement

### Types of FAI

**Cam Impingement**
- Extra bone on the femoral head (ball)
- Creates a bump that jams into socket
- More common in young athletic males
- Often from repetitive hip loading during growth

**Pincer Impingement**
- Extra bone on acetabulum (socket)
- Socket covers too much of the ball
- More common in middle-aged females
- Often from deeper hip socket

**Mixed Type**
- Combination of cam and pincer
- Most common presentation (50-70%)

### What Causes Pain

Impingement itself doesn't always cause pain—many people have FAI on imaging without symptoms. Pain occurs when:

- Abnormal contact irritates the labrum (cartilage ring)
- Repeated impingement causes inflammation
- Cartilage becomes damaged
- Surrounding muscles compensate and strain

### Symptoms

- Groin pain (most common location)
- Pain with hip flexion (bending hip up)
- Pain with prolonged sitting
- Clicking, catching, or locking
- Stiffness after inactivity
- Pain with squatting, lunging, or stairs
- Difficulty with certain positions
- Pain that limits sports participation

### The C-Sign

Patients often grab their hip in a "C" shape—thumb in groin, fingers on side of hip—indicating deep joint pain rather than surface muscle pain.

## Exercise Philosophy for FAI

### Key Principles

1. **Avoid end-range impingement positions** — Don't push into painful ranges
2. **Strengthen around the joint** — Hip and core muscles reduce joint stress
3. **Improve available mobility** — Within limits, not through them
4. **Modify problematic movements** — Find pain-free alternatives
5. **Progress gradually** — Build tolerance over time

### Positions to Be Cautious With

- Deep hip flexion (>90°)
- Hip flexion + internal rotation
- Deep squatting
- Certain yoga poses (pigeon, lotus)
- Prolonged sitting with crossed legs

## Phase 1: Pain Management and Mobility (Weeks 1-4)

### Activity Modification

**Reduce or modify:**
- Deep squats (stay above 90°)
- Hip flexor stretching to end range
- Positions that reproduce pain
- High-impact activities

**Maintain:**
- Walking
- Swimming
- Cycling (adjust seat height up)
- Strength training with modifications

### Gentle Hip Mobility

**Quadruped Rocking (Modified)**
1. On hands and knees
2. Rock hips back toward heels
3. Only go as far as comfortable (avoid pinching)
4. 10-15 repetitions
5. Gentle movement, not aggressive stretching

**Supine Hip Circles**
1. Lie on back
2. Bring knee toward chest (not all the way)
3. Make small circles with knee
4. 10 circles each direction
5. Explore pain-free range

**90-90 Hip Shifts**
1. Sit with both hips and knees at 90°
2. Shift weight from side to side
3. Maintain upright posture
4. 10-15 shifts
5. Works rotation without impingement

### Soft Tissue Work

**Hip Flexor Release**
1. Lie face down, lacrosse ball under front of hip
2. Apply gentle pressure
3. Make small movements over ball
4. 1-2 minutes per side
5. Avoid directly on bone

**Glute/Piriformis Release**
1. Sit on ball positioned in buttock
2. Cross ankle over opposite knee
3. Roll slowly over tight areas
4. 1-2 minutes per side

**TFL/IT Band Release**
1. Side-lying, roller under outer hip
2. Roll from hip to mid-thigh
3. 1-2 minutes
4. Can be tender—moderate pressure

### Gentle Stretching

**Hip Flexor Stretch (Modified)**
1. Half-kneeling position
2. Tuck pelvis under (posterior tilt)
3. Shift weight forward slightly
4. Don't force hip into extension
5. Hold 30 seconds

**Piriformis Stretch**
1. Lie on back, ankle over opposite knee
2. Pull bottom knee toward chest
3. Stay in pain-free range
4. Hold 30 seconds

**Adductor Stretch**
1. Stand with wide stance
2. Shift weight to one side
3. Keep feet flat
4. Hold 30 seconds each side

## Phase 2: Core and Hip Strengthening (Weeks 2-8)

Strong muscles unload the joint and control movement.

### Core Stabilization

**Dead Bug**
1. Lie on back, arms up, knees at 90°
2. Press low back into floor
3. Lower opposite arm and leg
4. Don't let back arch
5. 3 sets of 10 each side

**Bird Dog**
1. On hands and knees
2. Extend opposite arm and leg
3. Keep back flat
4. Hold 3-5 seconds
5. 3 sets of 10 each side

**Side Plank**
1. On elbow and knees (modified)
2. Keep body in straight line
3. Hold 20-30 seconds
4. 3 each side
5. Progress to full side plank

**Pallof Press**
1. Cable or band at chest height
2. Press straight out, resist rotation
3. Hold 10 seconds
4. 10 repetitions
5. Core anti-rotation for hip stability

### Gluteal Strengthening (Critical)

Strong glutes reduce hip joint stress and improve mechanics.

**Glute Bridge**
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat
2. Squeeze glutes, lift hips
3. Don't hyperextend back
4. Hold 3 seconds
5. 3 sets of 15

**Single-Leg Glute Bridge**
1. Same position, one leg extended
2. Bridge on single leg
3. Keep pelvis level
4. 3 sets of 10 each side

**Clamshell**
1. Side-lying, hips and knees bent 45°
2. Keep feet together, raise top knee
3. Don't rotate pelvis
4. 3 sets of 20 each side
5. Add band to progress

**Side-Lying Hip Abduction**
1. Lie on side, bottom leg bent
2. Raise top leg (straight) toward ceiling
3. Keep toes pointing forward
4. 3 sets of 15 each side

**Standing Hip Abduction**
1. Stand on one leg, hold support
2. Raise other leg out to side
3. Keep pelvis level
4. 3 sets of 15 each side
5. Add ankle weight to progress

**Hip Hike**
1. Stand on step, one leg hanging off
2. Drop hanging hip, then hike it up
3. Use stance-side glute medius
4. 3 sets of 15 each side

### Hip External Rotation Strengthening

**Seated External Rotation with Band**
1. Sit with band around knees
2. Press knees apart
3. Hold 5 seconds
4. 3 sets of 15

**Side-Lying External Rotation**
1. Lie on side, hip and knee at 90°
2. Keep knee in place, rotate foot toward ceiling
3. 3 sets of 15 each side

**Monster Walks**
1. Band around ankles
2. Quarter squat position
3. Walk sideways maintaining tension
4. 3 sets of 15 steps each direction

### Hip Extension Strengthening

**Prone Hip Extension**
1. Lie face down
2. Lift one leg straight up (squeeze glute)
3. Don't hyperextend back
4. 3 sets of 15 each side

**Quadruped Hip Extension**
1. On hands and knees
2. Extend one leg straight back
3. Keep back flat
4. 3 sets of 15 each side

**Romanian Deadlift (Bilateral)**
1. Hold weights in front
2. Hinge at hips, slight knee bend
3. Keep back flat
4. Go only to comfortable range
5. 3 sets of 12

## Phase 3: Functional Strengthening (Weeks 6-12)

### Modified Squatting

**Box Squat**
1. Squat to box (height that allows <90° hip flexion)
2. Sit back, touch box, stand up
3. Keep knees out
4. 3 sets of 12
5. Progress by lowering box gradually

**Goblet Squat (Elevated Heels)**
1. Hold weight at chest
2. Heels on small plates or wedge
3. Allows more upright torso
4. Squat to comfortable depth
5. 3 sets of 12

**Sumo Squat**
1. Wide stance, toes turned out
2. Squat between legs
3. May be more comfortable than regular squat
4. 3 sets of 12

### Hip Hinge Movements

**Kettlebell Deadlift**
1. Wide stance, KB between feet
2. Hinge at hips to grab KB
3. Drive hips forward to stand
4. 3 sets of 12

**Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift**
1. Stand on one leg
2. Hinge forward, other leg extends back
3. Stay in pain-free range
4. 3 sets of 10 each side

### Lunging (Modified)

**Reverse Lunge**
1. Step backward into lunge
2. Front knee stays behind toes
3. Don't let front hip flex past 90°
4. 3 sets of 10 each leg
5. Often better tolerated than forward lunge

**Lateral Lunge**
1. Step wide to the side
2. Sit hips back
3. Keep other leg straight
4. 3 sets of 10 each side

### Step Work

**Step-Ups**
1. Lower box height
2. Drive through top leg
3. Control descent
4. 3 sets of 12 each leg

**Lateral Step-Ups**
1. Step up from the side
2. Control movement
3. 3 sets of 10 each side

## Phase 4: Return to Activity (Weeks 10+)

### Sport-Specific Progression

**Criteria to progress:**
- Pain ≤2/10 with strength exercises
- Full strength routine without flare
- Single-leg exercises with good control
- Sport-specific movements without pain

**Gradual return:**
- Start with 50% intensity/volume
- Progress 10-20% per week
- Monitor 24-hour response
- Back off if pain increases

### Movement Modifications for Long-Term

**Squatting:**
- Limit depth if deep squatting causes pain
- Use wider stance
- Elevate heels
- Box squats for control

**Hip hinging:**
- Often well-tolerated
- Maintain form

**Running:**
- Usually fine if strength is adequate
- Address any gait issues

**Yoga:**
- Modify poses that cause impingement
- Props for support
- Avoid forcing positions

## Sample Weekly Programs

### Weeks 1-4: Mobility and Foundation
**Daily:**
- Gentle hip mobility: 5-10 min
- Soft tissue work: 5 min

**3× weekly:**
- Core exercises (dead bug, bird dog): 3 sets each
- Basic glute work (bridge, clamshell): 3 sets each
- Modified stretching: 2-3 × 30 sec each

### Weeks 4-8: Building Strength
**4× weekly:**
- Core circuit: 3 rounds
- Glute circuit (bridge, abduction, clamshell, hip hike): 3 sets each
- External rotation work: 3 sets

**Daily:**
- Brief mobility routine

### Weeks 8-12: Functional Progression
**4× weekly:**
- Modified squat pattern: 3 × 12
- Hip hinge: 3 × 12
- Single-leg work: 3 × 10 each
- Core maintenance

**2× weekly:**
- Sport-specific movements (as tolerated)

### Maintenance
**3× weekly:**
- Hip strengthening
- Core work

**As needed:**
- Mobility routine
- Activity modifications

## Lifestyle Modifications

### Sitting

- Avoid prolonged hip flexion
- Keep hips at or above knee level
- Take breaks every 30-45 min
- Avoid crossing legs

### Sleeping

- Side sleepers: pillow between knees
- Back sleepers: pillow under knees
- Avoid sleeping in tight hip flexion

### Exercise Modifications

**Cycling:**
- Raise seat height
- Reduces hip flexion angle

**Rowing:**
- May need to limit catch position
- Don't over-compress at the catch

**Yoga:**
- Modify deep hip flexion poses
- Use props
- Communicate with instructor

## When Surgery May Be Considered

**Conservative treatment fails if:**
- 3-6 months of dedicated exercise without improvement
- Significant labral damage on imaging
- Mechanical symptoms (catching, locking)
- Pain preventing desired activities

**Surgery options:**
- Hip arthroscopy (most common)
- Reshaping bone
- Labral repair
- 4-6 month recovery typically

**Note:** Research shows exercise-first approach is appropriate for most FAI patients. Many with abnormal imaging never develop symptoms.

## When to Seek Help

**See a healthcare provider if:**
- Severe pain limiting walking
- Mechanical symptoms (locking, catching)
- No improvement after 6-8 weeks of exercise
- Pain preventing sleep
- Considering activity avoidance

**Consider physical therapy for:**
- Supervised exercise progression
- Manual therapy
- Movement analysis
- Activity modification guidance

## Key Takeaways

1. **FAI doesn't always need surgery** — exercise works for most people
2. **Avoid impingement positions** — don't push into painful ranges
3. **Strengthen the hip muscles** — especially glutes and external rotators
4. **Core stability matters** — reduces joint stress
5. **Modify, don't avoid** — find pain-free alternatives
6. **Progress gradually** — build tolerance over time
7. **Positioning matters** — sitting, sleeping, exercise setup
8. **Be patient** — 3-6 months for significant improvement typical

Hip impingement can be well-managed with the right approach. Focus on strengthening around the joint, avoid positions that cause impingement, and modify activities rather than avoiding them. Most people with FAI can return to active lifestyles with proper rehabilitation.
