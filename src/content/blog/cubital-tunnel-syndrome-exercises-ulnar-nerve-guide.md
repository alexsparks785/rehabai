# Cubital Tunnel Syndrome Exercises: Complete Ulnar Nerve Relief Guide

Cubital tunnel syndrome is the second most common nerve entrapment (after carpal tunnel), causing numbness in the ring and pinky fingers, elbow pain, and hand weakness. The ulnar nerve gets compressed or stretched at the elbow, creating that uncomfortable "funny bone" sensation that doesn't go away.

The good news: most cases respond well to positioning changes, nerve gliding exercises, and activity modification—often avoiding surgery entirely.

## Understanding Cubital Tunnel Syndrome

### Anatomy

The ulnar nerve runs from your neck, behind the inside of your elbow (the cubital tunnel), and into your hand. At the elbow, it passes through a narrow tunnel between bone and ligament where it's vulnerable to:

- **Compression** — pressure on the nerve
- **Stretch** — nerve elongates when elbow bends
- **Friction** — nerve slides back and forth with elbow movement

### What Causes It

**Positional:**
- Prolonged elbow flexion (bent)
- Sleeping with elbows bent
- Leaning on elbows
- Phone use against ear

**Activities:**
- Repetitive elbow bending
- Throwing sports
- Weight lifting (certain exercises)
- Computer work with bent elbows

**Anatomical:**
- Shallow cubital tunnel
- Ulnar nerve subluxation (nerve pops over bone)
- Bone spurs or arthritis
- Previous elbow injury

### Symptoms

- Numbness/tingling in ring and pinky fingers
- Pain on inside of elbow
- Weakness in hand (especially grip)
- Difficulty with fine motor tasks
- Symptoms worse at night
- "Funny bone" sensation that doesn't go away
- Muscle wasting in severe cases (hand muscles)

### Cubital vs. Carpal Tunnel

| Feature | Cubital Tunnel | Carpal Tunnel |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Nerve | Ulnar | Median |
| Compression site | Elbow | Wrist |
| Affected fingers | Ring, pinky | Thumb, index, middle |
| Worse with | Elbow bent | Wrist bent |

## Phase 1: Protection and Positioning (Weeks 1-4)

Positioning changes are the most important first step.

### Elbow Positioning

**The Goal:** Keep elbow as straight as possible, especially at night.

**Night Splinting**
1. Wear elbow splint or brace at night
2. Keeps elbow at 30-45° (not fully bent)
3. Can use towel wrap as alternative:
   - Wrap towel around elbow
   - Secure with tape or sleeve
   - Prevents full bending during sleep

**Daytime Awareness**
- Avoid leaning on elbows
- Keep elbows straight when possible
- Phone: use speaker or earbuds
- Computer: elbows at 90° or less flexion

### Activity Modification

**Avoid or modify:**
- Prolonged elbow flexion
- Leaning on elbows
- Repetitive bending activities
- Direct pressure on inner elbow

**Workstation changes:**
- Keyboard at proper height
- Chair armrests padded or removed
- Monitor position (avoid chin on hand)
- Take frequent breaks

### Nerve Protection

**Elbow Pad**
- Wear pad over inner elbow
- Protects from direct pressure
- Useful if you must lean on elbows

## Phase 2: Nerve Gliding (Weeks 1-6)

Nerve glides help the ulnar nerve move freely through the cubital tunnel.

### Ulnar Nerve Glides

**Basic Ulnar Nerve Glide**
1. Stand or sit tall
2. Extend affected arm out to side, palm up
3. Bend wrist back (fingers toward ceiling)
4. Bend elbow, bringing hand toward shoulder
5. Straighten elbow while keeping wrist extended
6. 10-15 gentle repetitions
7. Should feel mild tension, not pain

**Alternative Ulnar Glide**
1. Arm at side, elbow straight
2. Bend wrist back, spread fingers
3. Tilt head toward same shoulder
4. Then tilt head away while flexing wrist
5. "Flossing" motion
6. 15-20 repetitions

**Seated Ulnar Nerve Glide**
1. Sit in chair, affected arm at side
2. Make "OK" sign with thumb and index finger
3. Bring hand up to make "glasses" over eye
4. Return to starting position
5. 10-15 repetitions
6. Classic nerve glide motion

### Progression

**Week 1-2:**
- Gentle, small movements
- 10 repetitions, 2-3× daily

**Week 3-4:**
- Increase range of motion
- 15 repetitions, 3× daily

**Week 5-6:**
- Full range as tolerated
- Continue maintenance

## Phase 3: Stretching and Mobility (Weeks 2-6)

### Forearm Stretches

**Wrist Flexor Stretch**
1. Extend arm in front, palm up
2. Use other hand to bend wrist down
3. Straighten elbow fully
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. Feel stretch in inner forearm

**Wrist Extensor Stretch**
1. Extend arm in front, palm down
2. Use other hand to bend wrist down
3. Hold 30 seconds
4. Feel stretch in outer forearm

### Neck Stretches

The ulnar nerve originates from the neck—tension there can contribute to symptoms.

**Lateral Neck Stretch**
1. Tilt ear toward shoulder
2. Gently pull head further with hand
3. Keep opposite shoulder down
4. Hold 30 seconds each side

**Upper Trap Stretch**
1. Same position as lateral neck stretch
2. Look down slightly toward armpit
3. Hold 30 seconds each side

### Elbow Mobility

**Elbow Flexion/Extension**
1. Gentle full range bending and straightening
2. 15-20 repetitions
3. Don't force at end ranges

**Forearm Rotation**
1. Elbow at side, bent 90°
2. Rotate palm up, then palm down
3. 15-20 repetitions

## Phase 4: Strengthening (Weeks 4-8)

As symptoms improve, strengthen the arm and hand.

### Grip Strengthening

**Stress Ball Squeeze**
1. Squeeze soft ball
2. Hold 5 seconds
3. 15-20 repetitions
4. Progress firmness

**Finger Pinch**
1. Putty between thumb and each finger
2. Pinch and hold
3. 10 repetitions per finger

### Hand Intrinsic Strengthening

Ulnar nerve innervates many small hand muscles.

**Finger Abduction with Band**
1. Rubber band around fingers
2. Spread fingers apart
3. Hold 3 seconds
4. 15-20 repetitions

**Finger Adduction**
1. Place paper between fingers
2. Squeeze fingers together, hold paper
3. Partner tries to pull paper out
4. 10 seconds hold, each space

**Thumb Adduction**
1. Paper between thumb and palm
2. Squeeze thumb against hand
3. Hold while partner pulls paper
4. 10 repetitions

### Wrist Strengthening

**Wrist Curls**
1. Forearm on table, palm up
2. Curl light weight up
3. Lower slowly
4. 3 sets of 15

**Reverse Wrist Curls**
1. Palm down
2. Extend wrist up
3. Lower slowly
4. 3 sets of 15

**Radial/Ulnar Deviation**
1. Hold weight, thumb up
2. Move wrist side to side
3. 3 sets of 15

### Forearm Strengthening

**Pronation/Supination with Weight**
1. Hold hammer or dumbbell at end
2. Rotate forearm fully each direction
3. 3 sets of 15

## Sample Programs

### Weeks 1-4: Protection Focus
**24/7:**
- Night splint or towel wrap
- Avoid prolonged elbow flexion
- Elbow pad if needed

**3× daily:**
- Gentle nerve glides: 10 reps
- Forearm stretches: 30 sec each

### Weeks 4-8: Mobility and Strengthening
**Ongoing:**
- Night positioning (may be able to wean)
- Activity modification

**Daily:**
- Nerve glides: 15 reps
- Stretches: 30 sec each

**3× weekly:**
- Grip strengthening: 3 × 15
- Hand intrinsic exercises: 3 × 15
- Wrist curls: 3 × 15

### Maintenance
**As needed:**
- Night splint if symptoms recur
- Nerve glides daily
- Strengthening 2-3× weekly
- Ongoing activity awareness

## Ergonomic Modifications

### Workstation

**Desk:**
- Elbows at 90° or slightly less
- Forearms supported
- Avoid leaning on elbows

**Chair:**
- Armrests padded or lowered
- Don't rest elbows on hard surfaces

**Keyboard/Mouse:**
- Elbows not hyperflexed
- Wrists neutral

### Phone Use

- Speaker phone or earbuds
- Avoid holding phone to ear (elbow bent)
- Switch hands regularly

### Sleeping

- **Essential:** Keep elbows straighter at night
- Night splint or towel wrap
- Avoid tucking hands under pillow
- Pillow between arms if side sleeping

### Driving

- Arms lower on steering wheel
- Elbows not resting on door
- Frequent position changes on long drives

## Exercises to Avoid or Modify

**Temporarily avoid:**
- Bicep curls (full flexion)
- Skull crushers
- Preacher curls
- Pull-ups (if symptomatic)
- Exercises with sustained elbow flexion

**Modifications:**
- Limit elbow flexion range
- Lighter weights, higher reps
- Padded surfaces for elbow contact
- Frequent breaks during sets

## When Conservative Treatment Fails

### Signs to Seek Medical Attention

**See a provider if:**
- No improvement after 6-8 weeks
- Progressive numbness or weakness
- Muscle wasting in hand
- Symptoms affecting daily function
- Dropping objects frequently

### Medical Options

**Diagnostic testing:**
- Nerve conduction study (NCS)
- Electromyography (EMG)
- Confirms diagnosis and severity

**Non-surgical:**
- Formal physical therapy
- Corticosteroid injection (limited evidence)
- Custom splinting

**Surgical options:**
- Cubital tunnel release
- Ulnar nerve transposition
- Recovery: 3-6 months typically
- Generally good outcomes for appropriate candidates

## Prevention Strategies

### Daily Habits

- Avoid prolonged elbow flexion
- Don't lean on elbows
- Take breaks from repetitive tasks
- Maintain good posture

### Exercise/Sports

- Warm up properly
- Avoid excessive elbow flexion under load
- Gradual progression of intensity
- Address any technique issues

### Sleep Hygiene

- Keep elbows straighter at night
- Good pillow and mattress support
- Avoid tucking arms under body

## Key Takeaways

1. **Positioning is crucial** — especially at night
2. **Keep elbows straighter** — the nerve stretches with flexion
3. **Night splinting works** — one of the most effective interventions
4. **Nerve glides help** — maintain neural mobility
5. **Avoid leaning on elbows** — direct pressure compresses the nerve
6. **Phone habits matter** — use speaker or earbuds
7. **6-8 weeks typical** — for improvement with conservative care
8. **Strengthening comes later** — after symptoms improve

Cubital tunnel syndrome can be very responsive to simple positioning changes and exercises. The key is reducing stretch and compression on the ulnar nerve, especially at night. Most people improve significantly within 6-8 weeks of consistent conservative treatment—many avoiding surgery entirely.
