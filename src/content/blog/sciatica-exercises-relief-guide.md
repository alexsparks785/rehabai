# Sciatica Exercises: Complete Relief and Recovery Guide

Sciatica—that shooting, burning pain running from your lower back down through your buttock and leg—affects up to 40% of people at some point in their lives. While the pain can be debilitating, the right exercises can provide significant relief and help prevent future episodes.

This guide covers evidence-based exercises for sciatica relief, organized by phase of recovery and type of sciatica you're experiencing.

## Understanding Your Sciatica

Before starting exercises, it helps to understand what's causing your sciatica:

### Disc-Related Sciatica (Most Common)
- Herniated or bulging disc pressing on nerve root
- Usually worse with sitting, bending forward
- May improve with standing or walking
- Often affects one side

### Piriformis Syndrome
- Piriformis muscle compressing sciatic nerve
- Worse with prolonged sitting
- Pain often centered in buttock
- May worsen with hip rotation activities

### Spinal Stenosis
- Narrowed spinal canal compressing nerves
- Usually affects people over 50
- Worse with standing and walking
- Relieved by sitting or bending forward

### Spondylolisthesis
- Vertebra slipping forward
- May cause bilateral symptoms
- Worse with extension (arching back)

**Important:** If you have severe weakness, bowel/bladder changes, or numbness in the saddle area, seek immediate medical attention.

## Phase 1: Acute Relief (Days 1-7)

When sciatica first flares, focus on reducing nerve irritation without aggravating symptoms.

### Nerve Glides (Neural Flossing)

Nerve glides help the sciatic nerve slide more freely through surrounding tissues.

**Seated Sciatic Nerve Glide**
1. Sit on chair edge, feet flat on floor
2. Slump your upper back slightly
3. Straighten the affected leg while pointing toes toward ceiling
4. As you straighten leg, look up toward ceiling
5. As you bend knee back down, tuck chin to chest
6. This creates a "flossing" motion for the nerve
7. Perform 10-15 gentle repetitions
8. Movement should be smooth, never forced

**Supine Nerve Glide**
1. Lie on back, affected leg toward ceiling (use strap if needed)
2. Keep knee slightly bent
3. Point and flex foot while holding leg position
4. Start with small movements, progress range as tolerated
5. 15-20 repetitions, 2-3 times daily

### Gentle Mobility

**Knee-to-Chest Stretch**
1. Lie on back
2. Bring one knee toward chest (start with unaffected side)
3. Hold 20-30 seconds
4. Alternate sides
5. Progress to both knees if tolerated

**Supine Spinal Twist (Modified)**
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat
2. Let both knees fall gently to one side
3. Keep within comfortable range
4. Hold 20-30 seconds
5. Return to center, repeat other side

**Cat-Cow (Gentle)**
1. On hands and knees
2. Slowly arch back (cow), then round back (cat)
3. Move through pain-free range only
4. 10 repetitions, focusing on gentle movement

### Positions of Relief

**Prone on Elbows (McKenzie Method)**
If your sciatica feels better with extension:
1. Lie face down
2. Prop onto elbows
3. Keep hips on ground
4. Hold 30 seconds to 2 minutes
5. If pain centralizes (moves toward back), this is positive

**Flexion Position**
If your sciatica feels better with flexion:
1. Lie on back
2. Bring both knees to chest
3. Gently rock side to side
4. Hold as long as comfortable

## Phase 2: Mobility Restoration (Weeks 2-4)

As acute pain decreases, focus on restoring normal movement and flexibility.

### Piriformis and Hip Stretches

**Figure-4 Stretch (Piriformis)**
1. Lie on back, both knees bent
2. Cross affected ankle over opposite knee
3. Pull bottom knee toward chest
4. Feel stretch deep in buttock
5. Hold 30-60 seconds
6. Can also do seated in chair

**Pigeon Pose (Modified)**
1. From hands and knees, bring affected leg forward
2. Knee points toward same-side hand
3. Lower body toward ground
4. Keep back leg extended
5. Only go as deep as comfortable
6. Hold 30-60 seconds

**Supine Piriformis Stretch**
1. Lie on back, affected knee bent, foot flat
2. Cross affected ankle over opposite knee
3. Rotate affected knee outward gently
4. Use hands to add gentle pressure if needed
5. Hold 30-60 seconds

### Hamstring Stretches

Tight hamstrings can increase tension on the sciatic nerve.

**Doorway Hamstring Stretch**
1. Lie in doorway, affected leg up on door frame
2. Opposite leg through doorway
3. Scoot closer to increase stretch
4. Keep knee slightly bent
5. Hold 30-60 seconds

**Standing Hamstring Stretch**
1. Place heel on low step or curb
2. Keep back straight, hinge at hips
3. Reach toward toes until stretch felt
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. Don't round lower back

### Hip Flexor Stretches

Tight hip flexors can increase lumbar lordosis and nerve compression.

**Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**
1. Kneel on affected side, other foot forward
2. Tuck pelvis under (posterior tilt)
3. Shift weight forward until stretch felt
4. Can raise same-side arm for more stretch
5. Hold 30-60 seconds

**Couch Stretch**
1. Face away from couch, one knee on cushion
2. Foot up against back of couch
3. Other foot on floor, knee at 90°
4. Squeeze glute on back leg
5. Hold 30-60 seconds

### Lumbar Mobility

**Prone Press-Up**
1. Lie face down, hands under shoulders
2. Press upper body up, keeping hips down
3. Go only as far as comfortable
4. Lower with control
5. 10 repetitions
6. Progress range gradually

**Lumbar Rotation Stretch**
1. Lie on back, knees bent
2. Let knees fall to one side
3. Keep shoulders on ground
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. Repeat other side

## Phase 3: Strengthening (Weeks 4-8)

Building core and hip strength protects against recurrence.

### Core Stabilization

**Dead Bug**
1. Lie on back, arms toward ceiling, knees at 90°
2. Press lower back into floor
3. Lower opposite arm and leg toward floor
4. Return to start
5. Alternate sides
6. 10 repetitions each side

**Bird Dog**
1. On hands and knees
2. Extend opposite arm and leg simultaneously
3. Keep back flat, don't rotate
4. Hold 3-5 seconds
5. Alternate sides
6. 10 repetitions each side

**Side Plank (Modified)**
1. Lie on side, bottom knee bent for support
2. Prop on bottom elbow
3. Lift hips off ground
4. Keep body in straight line from knees to shoulders
5. Hold 15-30 seconds
6. Progress to full side plank

**Pallof Press**
1. Stand sideways to cable or band
2. Hold at chest height
3. Press straight out and hold
4. Resist rotation
5. Hold 10 seconds, 10 repetitions
6. Repeat facing other direction

### Glute Strengthening

Weak glutes are strongly associated with sciatica recurrence.

**Glute Bridge**
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat
2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips
3. Create straight line from knees to shoulders
4. Hold 3-5 seconds
5. Lower with control
6. 15-20 repetitions

**Clamshell**
1. Lie on side, knees bent at 45°
2. Keep feet together
3. Lift top knee, keeping feet touching
4. Don't rotate pelvis
5. 15-20 repetitions each side
6. Add resistance band to progress

**Single-Leg Glute Bridge**
1. Lie on back, one foot flat, other leg extended
2. Drive through planted foot to lift hips
3. Keep pelvis level
4. 10-15 repetitions each side

**Hip Hike (Pelvic Drop)**
1. Stand on step, one foot hanging off edge
2. Let hanging hip drop down
3. Use stance-side glute to lift hip back up
4. 15 repetitions each side
5. Targets gluteus medius

### Back Strengthening

**Superman (Modified)**
1. Lie face down, arms extended
2. Lift just arms, or just legs, or opposite arm/leg
3. Hold 3-5 seconds
4. Progress to full superman
5. 10-15 repetitions

**Prone Hip Extension**
1. Lie face down
2. Keep leg straight, lift one leg a few inches
3. Squeeze glute at top
4. Hold 3 seconds
5. 15 repetitions each side

## Phase 4: Functional Integration (Weeks 8+)

Progress to functional movements that mimic daily activities.

### Movement Pattern Training

**Hip Hinge (Deadlift Pattern)**
1. Stand with slight knee bend
2. Push hips back, keeping back flat
3. Feel hamstring stretch
4. Return by squeezing glutes forward
5. Progress to adding light weight

**Split Stance Row**
1. Staggered stance, hinge forward at hips
2. Row dumbbell to hip
3. Maintains hip hinge under load
4. 10-12 repetitions each side

**Step-Ups**
1. Step onto box or stair
2. Drive through top foot
3. Control descent
4. Progress height and load
5. 10-15 repetitions each side

### Balance and Proprioception

**Single-Leg Balance**
1. Stand on one foot
2. Hold 30-60 seconds
3. Progress: eyes closed, unstable surface

**Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift**
1. Stand on one leg
2. Hinge forward, extending other leg back
3. Keep back flat
4. Touch ground or as low as controlled
5. 8-10 repetitions each side

## Exercise Selection by Sciatica Type

### For Disc-Related Sciatica

**Prioritize:**
- McKenzie extension exercises (prone press-ups)
- Nerve glides
- Core stabilization
- Glute strengthening

**Avoid initially:**
- Prolonged sitting
- Forward bending under load
- High-impact activities

### For Piriformis Syndrome

**Prioritize:**
- Piriformis stretches (figure-4, pigeon)
- Hip external rotation stretches
- Glute strengthening (especially gluteus medius)
- Hip mobility work

**Avoid initially:**
- Prolonged sitting
- Deep squatting
- Activities that compress piriformis

### For Spinal Stenosis

**Prioritize:**
- Flexion-based exercises
- Walking (especially uphill or on treadmill with incline)
- Core stabilization
- Stationary bike

**Avoid initially:**
- Extension exercises
- Prolonged standing
- Walking downhill

## Sample Weekly Program

### Acute Phase (Week 1)
**Daily:**
- Nerve glides: 2 sets × 15 reps
- Knee-to-chest: 3 × 30 seconds each side
- Position of relief: as needed
- Gentle walking: 5-10 minutes, multiple times daily

### Recovery Phase (Weeks 2-4)
**Daily:**
- Nerve glides: 2 × 15
- Piriformis stretch: 2 × 60 seconds
- Hip flexor stretch: 2 × 45 seconds
- Cat-cow: 10 repetitions

**3× weekly:**
- Dead bug: 3 × 10 each side
- Bird dog: 3 × 10 each side
- Glute bridge: 3 × 15
- Clamshell: 3 × 15 each side

### Strengthening Phase (Weeks 4-8)
**3-4× weekly:**
- Core circuit: Dead bug, bird dog, side plank (3 rounds)
- Glute circuit: Bridge, clamshell, hip hike (3 rounds)
- Stretching: Piriformis, hip flexor, hamstring (hold 60 seconds each)

**2× weekly:**
- Walking: 20-30 minutes
- Swimming or cycling: 20-30 minutes

### Maintenance Phase (Ongoing)
**3× weekly:**
- Full-body strength training including:
  - Hip hinge pattern
  - Single-leg work
  - Core anti-rotation
- Stretching routine
- 30+ minutes cardio

## Prevention Strategies

### Daily Habits

**Sitting:**
- Take breaks every 30-45 minutes
- Use lumbar support
- Keep feet flat on floor
- Don't cross legs for extended periods

**Lifting:**
- Bend at knees and hips
- Keep load close to body
- Don't twist while lifting
- Ask for help with heavy objects

**Sleeping:**
- Side sleepers: pillow between knees
- Back sleepers: pillow under knees
- Avoid stomach sleeping

### Long-Term Exercise

Maintain regular exercise including:
- Strength training 2-3× weekly
- Core work 2-3× weekly
- Regular stretching
- Cardiovascular exercise
- Avoid prolonged sedentary periods

## When to Seek Help

**See a healthcare provider if:**
- Symptoms persist beyond 4-6 weeks despite exercises
- Pain is severe and unresponsive to position changes
- You have progressive weakness in leg or foot
- Numbness is spreading or worsening
- You have difficulty controlling bladder or bowels
- Pain is associated with fever or unexplained weight loss

## Key Takeaways

1. **Identify your type** of sciatica—exercises differ based on cause
2. **Start gentle** with nerve glides and mobility in acute phase
3. **Progress systematically** through phases
4. **Strengthen consistently**—weak glutes and core are major risk factors
5. **Maintain mobility** in hips, hamstrings, and lumbar spine
6. **Stay active** within your limits—bed rest worsens outcomes
7. **Be patient**—most sciatica resolves within 6-12 weeks with appropriate care

The sciatic nerve is the longest nerve in your body. Treating it well with appropriate exercises, movement, and strengthening will help you recover from current symptoms and prevent future flare-ups.
