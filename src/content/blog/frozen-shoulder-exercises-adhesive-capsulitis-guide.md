# Frozen Shoulder Exercises: Complete Adhesive Capsulitis Recovery Guide

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) is one of the most frustrating conditions to experience—your shoulder progressively stiffens until basic movements like reaching overhead or behind your back become nearly impossible. The good news: frozen shoulder does resolve, and the right exercises can significantly speed recovery and reduce pain.

This guide covers evidence-based exercises for each stage of frozen shoulder, helping you understand what to expect and how to actively participate in your recovery.

## Understanding Frozen Shoulder

### What's Happening

Frozen shoulder involves thickening and tightening of the shoulder joint capsule—the connective tissue that surrounds the joint. This capsule normally allows smooth, full movement, but in frozen shoulder it becomes inflamed, then scarred and contracted.

**The three stages:**

1. **Freezing Stage (2-9 months)**
   - Gradual onset of pain
   - Pain often worse at night
   - Progressive loss of motion
   - Most painful stage

2. **Frozen Stage (4-12 months)**
   - Pain begins to decrease
   - Stiffness remains or worsens
   - Significant motion loss in all directions
   - Difficulty with daily activities

3. **Thawing Stage (6-24 months)**
   - Gradual return of motion
   - Pain continues to decrease
   - Can take many months to resolve
   - Active exercise speeds recovery

### Risk Factors
- Age 40-60
- Women more than men (2:1)
- Diabetes (10-20% of diabetics)
- Thyroid disorders
- Previous shoulder surgery or injury
- Prolonged immobilization
- Heart disease, stroke

### Characteristic Motion Loss

Frozen shoulder causes loss of both active AND passive motion—meaning even if someone else moves your arm, it won't go further. This distinguishes it from rotator cuff problems.

**Most affected movements:**
- External rotation (arm at side, rotate outward)
- Abduction (raising arm to side)
- Internal rotation (reaching behind back)

## Exercise Principles by Stage

### Freezing Stage
- **Goal:** Pain management, prevent further stiffening
- **Approach:** Gentle, within tolerance
- **Key:** Don't force through significant pain
- **Frequency:** Multiple times daily, short sessions

### Frozen Stage
- **Goal:** Maintain current motion, begin regaining range
- **Approach:** Progressive stretching at end-range
- **Key:** Consistent daily stretching
- **Frequency:** 2-3 sessions daily, longer holds

### Thawing Stage
- **Goal:** Restore full motion and strength
- **Approach:** More aggressive stretching, add strengthening
- **Key:** Push into discomfort (not sharp pain)
- **Frequency:** Daily stretching, 3x weekly strengthening

## Phase 1: Gentle Mobility (Freezing Stage)

### Pendulum Exercises

The cornerstone of early frozen shoulder exercise—uses gravity to gently mobilize the joint.

**Basic Pendulum**
1. Lean over with unaffected arm on table/chair
2. Let affected arm hang straight down
3. Relax shoulder completely
4. Gently swing arm in small circles
5. Let momentum do the work
6. 1-2 minutes clockwise, then counterclockwise
7. Can also swing forward-back, side-to-side
8. 3-4 times daily

**Weighted Pendulum**
1. Same position, hold 1-5 lb weight
2. Weight increases traction on joint
3. Perform same circular and linear swings
4. 2-3 minutes total
5. Progress weight as tolerated

### Passive Range of Motion

Have someone assist, or use your other arm.

**Assisted Forward Elevation**
1. Lie on back
2. Grasp affected wrist with good hand
3. Use good arm to lift affected arm toward ceiling, then overhead
4. Go to first point of resistance, not through sharp pain
5. Hold 5-10 seconds
6. 10-15 repetitions

**Table Slides**
1. Sit at table, affected forearm on towel
2. Slide arm forward on table
3. Let trunk lean forward to increase stretch
4. Hold 10-20 seconds at end range
5. 10-15 repetitions

### Gentle External Rotation

**Supine External Rotation**
1. Lie on back, affected elbow at side, bent 90°
2. Hold stick or cane with both hands
3. Use good arm to push affected arm outward
4. Keep elbow pinned to side
5. Go to first resistance, hold 5-10 seconds
6. 10-15 repetitions

## Phase 2: Progressive Stretching (Frozen Stage)

### Stretching Guidelines

- Hold stretches 30-60 seconds (longer holds are more effective)
- Perform 3-5 repetitions per stretch
- 2-3 sessions daily
- Discomfort acceptable (4-5/10), sharp pain means back off
- Use heat before and ice after if helpful

### External Rotation Stretches

**Doorway External Rotation**
1. Stand in doorway, elbow at 90°, forearm on door frame
2. Step forward through doorway
3. Feel stretch in front of shoulder
4. Hold 30-60 seconds
5. Progress by raising elbow height
6. 3-5 repetitions

**Supine External Rotation with Stick**
1. Lie on back, hold stick with both hands
2. Keep elbows at sides, bent 90°
3. Push affected arm outward using stick
4. Hold at maximum tolerable stretch
5. 30-60 seconds, 3-5 reps

**Side-Lying External Rotation Stretch**
1. Lie on affected side
2. Elbow bent 90°, resting on pillow
3. Use weight of unaffected hand to push forearm toward floor
4. Creates external rotation stretch
5. 30-60 seconds holds

### Cross-Body (Horizontal Adduction) Stretch

**Standing Cross-Body**
1. Bring affected arm across chest
2. Use other hand to pull elbow toward opposite shoulder
3. Keep shoulder down, don't shrug
4. Feel stretch in back of shoulder
5. Hold 30-60 seconds
6. 3-5 repetitions

### Overhead/Flexion Stretches

**Wall Slide/Walk**
1. Face wall, fingers of affected arm on wall
2. Walk fingers up the wall as high as possible
3. Hold at end range 15-30 seconds
4. Walk fingers back down
5. 10-15 repetitions
6. Mark progress with tape

**Doorway Flexion Stretch**
1. Hold doorframe overhead with affected arm
2. Step forward to increase stretch
3. Hold 30-60 seconds
4. Keep arm straight
5. 3-5 repetitions

**Supine Flexion with Towel**
1. Lie on back, hold towel behind you with both hands
2. Good arm overhead, affected arm at side
3. Pull towel to raise affected arm overhead
4. Hold 30-60 seconds at end range

### Internal Rotation Stretches

**Towel Stretch (Behind Back)**
1. Hold towel behind back—good hand overhead, affected hand at low back
2. Gently pull upward with good hand
3. Feel stretch in front of affected shoulder
4. Hold 30-60 seconds
5. 3-5 repetitions
6. Progress reach gradually

**Sleeper Stretch**
1. Lie on affected side, shoulder and elbow at 90°
2. Use other hand to push forearm toward floor
3. Feel stretch in back of shoulder
4. Hold 30 seconds
5. Controversial—skip if causes pain

## Phase 3: Advanced Stretching and Strengthening (Thawing Stage)

### Aggressive Stretching

**End-Range Holds**
- All previous stretches with longer holds (60-90 seconds)
- Push further into range
- Multiple repetitions
- Heat before, ice after

**Contract-Relax Technique (PNF)**
1. Stretch to end range
2. Gently push against resistance for 5-10 seconds (isometric)
3. Relax and stretch further
4. Repeat 3-4 times per stretch
5. Can significantly improve range

### Strengthening Exercises

Begin strengthening as range improves and pain decreases.

**Isometrics (Start Here)**

*External Rotation Isometric*
1. Stand with elbow at side, bent 90°, next to wall
2. Push back of hand into wall
3. Hold 10 seconds
4. 10 repetitions

*Flexion Isometric*
1. Face wall, fist against wall at shoulder height
2. Push forward into wall
3. Hold 10 seconds
4. 10 repetitions

**Resistance Band Exercises**

*External Rotation*
1. Band anchored at waist height
2. Elbow at side, bent 90°
3. Rotate forearm outward against band
4. Control return
5. 3 sets of 15

*Internal Rotation*
1. Same setup, turn to face other direction
2. Rotate forearm across body against band
3. Control return
4. 3 sets of 15

*Rows*
1. Face anchor point
2. Pull band toward waist
3. Squeeze shoulder blades
4. 3 sets of 15

**Shoulder Strengthening**

*Prone Y-T-W*
1. Lie face down, arms hanging off edge
2. Y: Raise arms at 45° angle, thumbs up
3. T: Raise arms straight to sides
4. W: Elbows bent, squeeze shoulder blades
5. 10 repetitions each position

*Side-Lying External Rotation*
1. Lie on unaffected side
2. Affected elbow at side, bent 90°
3. Rotate forearm toward ceiling
4. Lower with control
5. 3 sets of 15
6. Progress with light weight

### Functional Movement Integration

**Reaching Activities**
- Practice reaching overhead for objects
- Reaching behind back
- Reaching across body
- Progress height/distance gradually

**Daily Activity Simulation**
- Hair washing motions
- Putting on jacket
- Reaching for seatbelt
- Reaching into cabinets

## Sample Programs by Stage

### Freezing Stage (Focus: Pain Management)
**3-4× daily:**
- Pendulum exercises: 2-3 minutes
- Passive forward elevation: 10-15 reps
- Gentle external rotation: 10-15 reps
- Heat before, ice after if helpful

### Frozen Stage (Focus: Regain Range)
**2-3× daily:**
- Pendulum warm-up: 2 minutes
- All stretches: 30-60 second holds, 3-5 reps each
- External rotation (doorway)
- Cross-body stretch
- Overhead wall slide
- Internal rotation towel stretch

### Thawing Stage (Focus: Restore Function)
**Daily:**
- Complete stretching routine with longer holds

**3× weekly:**
- Isometrics: 10 reps each position
- Band exercises: 3 × 15 each direction
- Y-T-W: 10 each position
- Side-lying external rotation: 3 × 15

## Adjunct Treatments

### Heat Therapy
- Use before exercise to increase tissue flexibility
- 15-20 minutes warm pack or shower
- Particularly helpful in frozen stage

### Ice Therapy
- Use after exercise if significant inflammation
- 15-20 minutes
- Most helpful in freezing stage

### Manual Therapy
- Joint mobilization by physical therapist
- Soft tissue massage
- Can accelerate range gains

### Medical Interventions
- **Corticosteroid injection:** Can reduce pain in freezing stage
- **Hydrodilatation:** Injection to stretch capsule
- **Manipulation under anesthesia:** For refractory cases
- **Arthroscopic capsular release:** Surgical option

## Timeline Expectations

Frozen shoulder typically resolves in 1-3 years, but active exercise can significantly shorten this.

**With consistent exercise:**
- Freezing stage: 2-4 months
- Frozen stage: 4-8 months
- Thawing stage: 4-12 months
- Full recovery: 12-24 months total

**Factors affecting recovery:**
- Diabetes (longer recovery)
- Severity at diagnosis
- Consistency with exercises
- Previous shoulder problems
- Response to treatment

## Common Mistakes

### Too Aggressive Too Early
- Forcing through sharp pain
- High-intensity stretching in freezing stage
- Can worsen inflammation and slow recovery

### Not Aggressive Enough in Thawing
- Only doing gentle exercises when ready for more
- Not pushing into stretch
- Accepting limited range as "normal"

### Inconsistency
- Sporadic exercise
- Skipping days
- Not performing enough repetitions or duration

### Neglecting Strengthening
- Only stretching, never strengthening
- Weak muscles can't support regained range
- Risk of re-stiffening

## When to Seek Help

**See a healthcare provider if:**
- Pain is severe and unmanaged
- No improvement after 4-6 weeks of consistent exercise
- Significant weakness
- Symptoms in both shoulders
- Suspected fracture or dislocation
- Night pain disrupting sleep consistently

**Consider physical therapy for:**
- Supervised progression
- Manual therapy to complement exercise
- Guidance on when to advance
- Motivation and accountability

## Key Takeaways

1. **Frozen shoulder has three stages** — exercise approach differs for each
2. **Pendulum exercises** are essential throughout, especially early
3. **Stretching requires long holds** — 30-60+ seconds, multiple reps
4. **Discomfort is acceptable** — sharp pain means back off
5. **Consistency is critical** — daily exercises, 2-3 sessions
6. **Strengthen as range returns** — don't neglect this phase
7. **Be patient** — recovery takes months, but it DOES happen
8. **Heat before, ice after** — can improve session effectiveness

Frozen shoulder is challenging and slow, but it's also self-limiting—it WILL get better. Your active participation in exercises can significantly speed recovery and improve your final outcome. Stay consistent, be patient, and trust the process.
